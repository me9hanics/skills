# Brief factual reporting

Use this mode for concise reports and updates where the point is who reports what. Its characteristic sentence shape is **source + neutral reporting verb + concrete development or topic**. Attribution is part of the information, not decorative framing.

User-supplied style examples, reproduced as examples rather than independently verified current news:

- Börsianer reports that RBI expects a court ruling in the Rasperia case soon.
- Several media outlets report that Burtscher was appointed as CRO of RLB Tirol.
- AFP reports on the negotiations about the new EU sanctions package.

## Sentence construction

- Lead with the source when it matters who is reporting. Use "reports that" for a reported claim and "reports on" for coverage of a topic. Preserve the actual source's stance when another verb is needed, such as "estimates", "expects", or "alleges"; these are not interchangeable synonyms.
- Follow with a direct, concrete statement: actor, action or development, and the context needed to identify it. Prefer a short declarative sentence with little introductory or evaluative wording.
- Preserve nested attribution and uncertainty. "Börsianer reports that RBI expects a ruling soon" identifies the reporting outlet, the holder of the expectation, and the expected event. "The court will rule soon" loses that structure and strengthens the claim.
- Keep event stage and timing intact: negotiations remain negotiations, a proposal remains a proposal, and an appointment is distinct from a nomination. Preserve names, roles, organizations, and other relevant identifiers.
- Attribute only what the supplied evidence supports. "Several media outlets" is appropriate when the material establishes multiple outlets; never invent corroboration. This mode shapes prose and does not itself verify the reported event.
- Keep explanation or personal interpretation separate when requested. A short attributed report generally needs no added commentary, significance claim, or reflection.

## Format

Bullets work well for independent items; the same sentence style can appear in ordinary paragraphs or a briefing. Choose the format for the document rather than forcing bullets. Keep useful consistency between items, without flattening differences in source, certainty, or event status. Use another sentence when a relevant qualification will not fit clearly into the first.
