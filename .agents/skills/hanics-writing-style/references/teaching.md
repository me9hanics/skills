# Teaching and explanation

Use the smallest explanation or representation that makes the concept understandable. This guidance adapts the user's suggested `show-me` inspiration to authored documents; it does not require that skill or a particular visualization tool.

- Identify what the audience needs to understand and the missing conceptual steps. Supply only the background those steps require.
- Introduce an idea through a concrete example, mechanism, or useful distinction, then connect it to the terminology. Keep an established term when the audience already understands it.
- Choose a representation for what it reveals: a worked example for application, pseudocode for logic, a flow for sequence, a tree for hierarchy, or a table for comparable alternatives. Plain prose is sufficient when it makes the relationship clear.
- Keep each example or visual next to the text explaining its point. Include enough context that omitted details do not obscure order, responsibility, or causality.
- Compress repetition and framing while preserving the reasoning steps the reader needs. A terse definition is insufficient when the task is to explain how or why.
- Mark hypothetical examples as such. Preserve an analogy's useful correspondence without implying the two things are identical.

Use personal reflection when supplied and helpful to understanding, for example why an earlier interpretation failed. Do not invent an autobiographical discovery story to make an explanation feel personal.
