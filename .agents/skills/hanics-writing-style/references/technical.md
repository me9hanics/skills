# Technical descriptions

Give the reader a compact account of what the thing is, how it works, and why its relevant choices matter. Match detail to the document's purpose and audience while preserving the source information within scope.

- Organize around the actual relationships: process order, inputs and outputs, responsibilities, dependencies, tradeoffs, or comparisons. Use only the structure that helps the reader follow them.
- Retain mechanisms and concrete distinctions. "Automates the workflow" cannot replace a description of which steps are automated, where judgment remains, and what is produced.
- Keep constraints, exceptions, assumptions, and reasons beside the statements they qualify. Distinguish current behavior, proposed changes, and open decisions.
- Explain unfamiliar terms where needed; use established terminology consistently. Prefer ordinary language where a specialized term adds no precision.
- Use prose for connected reasoning, lists for parallel facts, and tables for recurring comparison dimensions. Avoid duplicating the same information in an introduction, body, and closing summary unless the requested format needs that repetition.
- Personal reflection belongs when the document includes the author's reasoning or lessons learned. Otherwise let the technical explanation carry the voice through clarity and density.

Apply the main skill's compression check to the full information within scope. A compact representation still needs to expose relevant conditions and relationships rather than leave the reader to reconstruct them.
