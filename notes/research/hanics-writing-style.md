## Tensions to resolve in the design

- The old AI description favors full, well-balanced sentences and smooth academic phrasing ([lines 3–6](C:/GitHubRepo/REPOS/cv-automation/personal/writing_style.md:3), [21–25](C:/GitHubRepo/REPOS/cv-automation/personal/writing_style.md:21)). Current instructions explicitly permit word omission. Proposed resolution: clarity and preservation of information first; complete sentences when useful, compact omission or fragments when natural for the format. Do not automatically polish away the user's compactness.
- The old AI description says reflection can soften impact and suggests stronger assertion ([lines 44–47](C:/GitHubRepo/REPOS/cv-automation/personal/writing_style.md:44)). This is past assistant advice, not an accepted request to remove reflection. Current instruction favors reflection. Proposed resolution: remove empty hedging, preserve meaningful epistemic qualification and authentic reflection.
- A cover letter selects relevant evidence from a broad life history; compression of already-selected source text must retain every relevant informative statement. Proposed resolution: distinguish **selection for relevance** from **compression after selection**. Do not silently declare an awkward fact irrelevant just to meet a length target.
- `sentences-rephrase` is deliberately sentence-local; `hanics-writing-style` should be able to compose and restructure unless the user limits the edit. When both apply, the explicit edit scope controls.
- Installed [writing-beats](C:/Users/hanic/.agents/skills/writing-beats/SKILL.md:58) permits unused fragments and [writing-shape](C:/Users/hanic/.agents/skills/writing-shape/SKILL.md:43) recommends cutting paragraphs that do not earn a place. These are generic composition workflows. They must not override this user's explicit preservation requirement.
- `show-me` explains concepts to the user during a discussion. Here its transferable principle is economical representation for the document's audience. Do not import a teaching workspace, repeated questions, HTML generation, or every possible visualization by default. The installed `teach` skill's multi-session workflow is outside this writing-style task.
- Compression is an operation across genres, not a separate genre. An email may need compression and persuasion; a technical document may both describe and teach. Fixed signatures, dates, page lengths, and rendering commands in the CV repository are delivery-workflow constraints, not reusable style defaults.

## Proposed skill design

**One skill, `hanics-writing-style`, with a small common core and optional genre references.** Trigger it when writing or rewriting professional documents or messages in Hanics's style. Do not let it govern ordinary discussion with an assistant. Infer audience, purpose, source material, and document type from the request; ask only when a missing answer materially affects the writing.

Resolve evidence conflicts in this order: current user instructions → explicit applicable genre instructions → user-authored or user-approved professional examples → older AI descriptions and templates. Treat compression as a cross-cutting operation. Avoid creating a large menu of rigid presets.

The common core should encode these observable principles:

1. Write directly and concretely. Prefer ordinary verbs and specific claims to corporate phrasing, hype, ornamental transitions, or inflated achievements.
2. Preserve the writer's actual reasoning: what happened, why it mattered, what it taught or changed, and why it is relevant here. Include personal reflection when supported and useful, without forcing autobiographical commentary into impersonal technical documentation.
3. Use dense but readable phrasing. Delete empty scaffolding, repeated context, and redundant words before removing substantive content. Recombine clauses or use compact lists/tables when the task allows. Avoid needless stylistic polish.
4. Preserve factual distinctions: actors, actions, quantities, conditions, exceptions, time, uncertainty, causal relationships, and attribution. Do not replace a precise statement with a broader but less informative label.
5. Keep the text self-contained for its intended audience. Explain necessary background compactly; do not repeat concepts the audience already understands.
6. State supported contributions honestly and directly. Keep genuine limits and uncertainty. Do not invent experience, causal links, motivations, outcomes, or polished personal reflections.

Genre guidance should be short:

- **Cover letters and outreach:** establish purpose early; select experiences relevant to this particular recipient; connect experience, insight, and relevance in a coherent sequence; distinguish current capability from adjacent experience and learning intention; close with a natural next step. Use the current local punctuation and rhythm preferences as initial cover-letter rules. Avoid rigid paragraph counts and stock enthusiasm.
- **Technical descriptions:** retain mechanisms, interfaces, constraints, caveats, and reasons at the level the source supports. Select structure based on the relationships: prose for reasoning, lists for parallel facts, tables for repeated comparisons. Omit decorative summaries and sections that duplicate content.
- **Explanations and teaching:** establish the idea before relying on its terminology; use a concrete example or minimal visual representation when that makes the concept easier to understand. Preserve necessary conceptual steps even when compressing. This is proposed pedagogy informed by `show-me`, not verified evidence of the user's teaching voice.

A lightweight internal check should protect compression: enumerate the source's informative statements, mark actual duplicates and genuinely irrelevant material, draft, then map every retained statement back to an explicit passage in the result. Check qualifications and relationships as well as named facts. If a hard length limit cannot accommodate all relevant statements, surface the conflict; do not silently convert compression into a lossy summary. The check need not be exposed as a separate artifact during normal use.

Suggested future packaging: a concise `SKILL.md`, conditional references for applications/correspondence, technical description, and teaching, plus a small evidence/example reference. Keep compression in the core, extracting its detailed procedure only if substantial. Examples should carry provenance (user-authored, user-approved, or illustrative). Do not copy personal CV details into the reusable core or create a large adjective-based personality profile.

## Remaining gaps

- Exact cover-letter/storytelling corrections from prior ChatGPT chats are not established by local files. They may refine or supersede the older templates.
- The retrieved chats provide user-authored professional prose and explicit editing requests, but no sampled local professional file has independently verified human authorship or explicit approval history. A confirmed final letter and a longer confirmed technical document would improve calibration further.
- The generality of the cover-letter punctuation rules remains uncertain. Most local evidence is English; Hungarian or other-language stylistic conventions have not been established.
- There is more evidence for application/outreach writing than for technical compression or teaching. The latter principles should be labeled proposals based on current instructions, not claimed discoveries of a proven historical style.
- No compression ratio or universal target sentence length is supported. Compression should be constrained by preserved information and readability, rather than a fixed percentage.

Research recommendation: propose the compact core plus three genre modes now; use the user's reactions and confirmed exemplars to sharpen it before implementation. Do not turn this research into a broad editing, publishing, research, or teaching workflow.
